    <span className="sub-heading">Dark Themes</span>
    <p>A dark theme usually inverts the scales: <code>white</code> becomes the dark surface (cards, inputs, modals), <code>black</code> the brightest text, and the tints and shades swap places. In an inverted theme, <code>white</code> and <code>black</code> name the surface and text roles, not the colors themselves.</p>
    <p>Text and icons over a solid fill don't follow the surface: they follow the fill. White text on a <code>#3366FF</code> button still reads best when the page turns dark. That's why components use the <code>on</code> colors there instead of <code>white</code>:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><code>onPrimary</code>: filled primary buttons, checked checkboxes, radios and toggles, selected dates and the MultiSelect counter.</li>
      <li><code>onAccent</code> / <code>onNeutral</code>: tertiary buttons, the MultiSelect counter and today's date, over the accent color when the theme defines one and over neutral otherwise.</li>
      <li><code>onSuccess</code> / <code>onDanger</code>: filled success and danger buttons.</li>
    </ul>
    <p>The <code>on</code> colors are white by default and are never inverted. In a dark theme, set them for each fill: keep them light when the fill is still dark enough, make them dark when the fill is light (e.g. a pastel pink or green).</p>
    <p>Set them as hex values, not as <code>$white</code> / <code>$black</code>: those point to the theme's own <code>white</code> and <code>black</code>, which are inverted. In a dark theme, <code>'$black'</code> is the light text color.</p>
